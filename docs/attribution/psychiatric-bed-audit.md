# Audit of adult and child inpatient psychiatric capacity

On July 1, 2026, the 21 mapped campuses with a documented adult allocation accounted for **1,879 adult psychiatric beds**. Eight had dedicated child/adolescent beds, totaling **230**. The other two campuses, Columbia and Allen, share a Manhattan hospital reporting row with Weill Cornell. Their July adult allocations are unknown. All 23 have a documented child/adolescent count, including explicit zeros. [OMH Table 5, NYC](https://omh.ny.gov/omhweb/transformation/docs/omh-monthly-report-aug-2026.pdf#page=23) · [OMH Table 5, Westchester](https://omh.ny.gov/omhweb/transformation/docs/omh-monthly-report-aug-2026.pdf#page=22)

## What the numbers count

The state labels the three capacity columns “Total,” “Adults” and “Child.” They cover hospital inpatient psychiatric programs and include psychiatric specialties. The other six columns in Table 5 report percentages from an earlier discharge cohort. Footnote 6 applies to the discharge statistics. It counts people age 21 or younger on child/adolescent units as children and people age 16 or older on adult units as adults. A particular unit’s admission ages, specialty, staffing and open beds require separate evidence. [Table 5 dates and headers](https://omh.ny.gov/omhweb/transformation/docs/omh-monthly-report-aug-2026.pdf#page=23) · [Table 5 notes](https://omh.ny.gov/omhweb/transformation/docs/omh-monthly-report-aug-2026.pdf#page=24)

Bellevue gives a direct check on the distinction between psychiatric beds and the whole hospital. A state review dated July 9 describes **over 800 hospital beds overall** and **316 OMH-licensed inpatient psychiatric beds** across 12 units serving adults, older adults, children, adolescents and forensic patients. The state table assigns 271 of the 316 to adults. Bellevue describes a 15-bed children's unit and two 15-bed adolescent units, which independently reproduce the 45 child beds. Its pediatric psychiatric emergency service has six separate extended-observation beds. In September the city funded 40 additional adult psychiatric beds as a future expansion. [OMH Bellevue project 237, printed p. 13](https://omh.ny.gov/omhweb/bh_services_council/bhsac-070926-omh-projects.pdf) · [Bellevue clinical services](https://www.nychealthandhospitals.org/bellevue/services/behavioral-health/) · [September funding announcement](https://www.nychealthandhospitals.org/report-to-the-board-of-directors-sep-2026/)

The eleven NYC Health + Hospitals campuses contribute 1,157 adult and 134 child beds to the July figures. The mapped White Plains campus contributes 188 adult and 45 child. The subtotal for mapped campuses within NYC is therefore 1,691 known adult and 185 child beds. For context, summing **all 31 NYC reporting rows** in Table 5 yields 2,514 adult and 240 child. The statewide Table 5 totals are 4,907 adult and 809 child across general and private hospitals. State-operated psychiatric centers are covered separately in Table 1. ## How shared and renamed rows were resolved

- **Mount Sinai.** The 64-bed permanent psychiatric certificate identifies 45 Rivington Street, which OMH lists under Beth Israel. The operator identifies detoxification and substance-use rehabilitation as separate inpatient programs. The St. Luke’s–Roosevelt row covers Morningside and West. The site certificates sum to 106 (74 + 32) and Mount Sinai places all 19 child beds at Morningside. Subtraction produces the 55 and 32 adult allocations. [Rivington inpatient programs](https://www.mountsinai.org/locations/behavioral-health-center/patients/inpatient) · [Morningside pediatric inpatient unit](https://www.mountsinai.org/locations/child-behavioral-health-center/emergency-inpatient)
- **NewYork-Presbyterian.** The Manhattan row reports 85 adults and zero children. The April agreement and site certification identify 36 adult beds at Weill Cornell. The other 49 remain visible in the reporting-entity figure. Columbia’s project described temporarily relocating 24 beds to Allen during renovation. The legal agreement sets targets subject to regulatory approval. Those records do not establish the July split. White Plains is a separate county row. [Attorney General agreement, paragraphs 76–78](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/settlements-agreements/new-york-v-the-newyork-presbyterian-hospital-assurance-of-discontinuance-2026.pdf#page=45) · [OMH Columbia project](https://omh.ny.gov/omhweb/bh_services_council/bhsac-052725-omh_projects.pdf#page=2) · [OMH Westchester row](https://omh.ny.gov/omhweb/transformation/docs/omh-monthly-report-aug-2026.pdf#page=22)
- **Zucker Hillside.** The LIJ certificate records 236 under the general hospital ID. OMH’s project specifies the two Glen Oaks addresses for the 226-bed psychiatric facility and its ten-bed adult expansion. The September 2025 report confirms implementation on July 14, 2025. Only this psychiatric program is attributed to Zucker. [Project 151](https://oasas.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/09/bhsac-omh-projects-9.11.24.pdf#page=5) · [Recorded change](https://omh.ny.gov/omhweb/transformation/docs/omh-monthly-report-sep-2025.pdf#page=24) · [LIJ certificate](https://health.data.ny.gov/resource/2g9y-7kqm.json?fac_id=1630&attribute_type=Bed&attribute_value=Psychiatric)
- **Montefiore.** The network reports 55 adults and 21 children in the Bronx. Montefiore identifies 22 adult beds at Moses and 33 at Wakefield. The state identifies the separate NYC Children's Center campus for the 21 youth beds. Only the 22 adult beds belong to the mapped Moses campus. [Montefiore inpatient program](https://montefioreeinstein.org/patient-care/services/psychiatry/programs/inpatient-psychiatry?fp-link-id=92hzu5gb) · [Children's center opening](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/mental-health-milestone-governor-hochul-announces-1000-psychiatric-beds-opened-across-new-york)
- **Staten Island.** Richmond identifies the Bard Avenue psychiatric inpatient service with 30 adult and ten youth beds. The state profile for Prince’s Bay identifies the 35 psychiatric beds assigned to Staten Island University Hospital’s reporting row. [Richmond service](https://rumcsi.org/services/psychiatric-emergency-services/) · [Prince’s Bay DOH profile](https://profiles.health.ny.gov/hospital/printview/106819)

## Operational availability

NYC Health + Hospitals reported a 2025 average of approximately 1,090 psychiatric beds online systemwide and an average daily inpatient census of approximately 950. These annual averages cover all ages. Obtaining an individual hospital's openings for an adult or child transfer requires time-stamped operational information about staffed units, occupied beds, holds and admission restrictions. New York describes a psychiatric Bed Availability System for participating hospitals, OMH field offices and county mental health directors using HERDS within the Health Commerce System. The public daily hospital capacity dashboard covers adult acute and ICU beds across clinical services.
